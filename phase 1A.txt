Executive Summary & Overall Feasibility VerdictBuilding a private, personal-use iPhone 14 Pro music application on iOS 26.5 from a Windows 11 PC at a strict €0.00 ($0.00) total cost—without owning a Mac, renting cloud macOS hardware, or paying $99/year for the Apple Developer Program—is technically achievable, provided the architecture respects three hard boundaries enforced by Apple and Spotify:Zero-Cost Compilation & Signing: You can write Native Swift 6 + SwiftUI (targeting iOS 26 SDK) in Visual Studio Code on Windows 11, compile unsigned .ipa bundles in the cloud for €0 using GitHub Actions macos-latest (Xcode 26) runners, and sign/install them onto your iPhone 14 Pro via SideStore + LocalDevVPN using a free Personal Team Apple ID.Spotify Playback Ownership Model: Under Spotify's Developer Terms and the Development Mode restrictions, third-party apps cannot decrypt or stream raw Spotify audio frames directly into a custom audio buffer. Instead, the official Spotify iOS app acts as the local background audio engine, while your app acts as a low-latency Hybrid Controller & Visualizer (combining the Spotify iOS App Remote SDK for zero-latency local IPC state/control with the Spotify Web API for library/search/metadata). Note: Spotify's Development Mode rules require the developer account owner to hold an active Spotify Premium subscription to register/operate a Web API Client ID.System Lock Screen & Bundle ID Conservation: Because the official Spotify app owns the active AVAudioSession, iOS 26 grants Spotify—not your companion app—ownership of the system Lock Screen MPNowPlayingInfoCenter card and system Dynamic Island audio pill. Furthermore, because a free Apple ID permits only 3 active sideloaded app/extension bundle IDs (where SideStore takes 1 slot and your Music App takes 1 slot), adding a WidgetKit/Live Activity extension would consume your 3rd and final slot. Therefore, the core architecture ships as a Single-Bundle-ID Native SwiftUI App with an optional compile-time flag (ENABLE_LIVE_ACTIVITY_EXTENSION) reserved for when you have a free slot available.2. Only the application currently holding an active, playing AVAudioSession with category .playback is displayed on the Lock Screen and inside the system Dynamic Island Now Playing pill.What happens if your app plays a silent audio track to steal the Lock Screen? If your app activates a non-mixable .playback session, it immediately interrupts and pauses Spotify's audio. If your app uses .mixWithOthers, iOS ignores its MPNowPlayingInfoCenter dictionary in favor of the non-mixable primary audio app (Spotify).The Honest Engineering Solution: Let the official Spotify iOS app own the system Lock Screen and Control Center audio session naturally.
